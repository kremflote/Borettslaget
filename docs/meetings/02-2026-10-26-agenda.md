### 3. Solution directions: prompts

The company's ideas (not requirements): visual navigation in the building; recommendations from needs and life situation; an AI assistant answering questions about units. Plus our own. For each, ask:
- Which buyer problem does it solve: finding, comparing, understanding (sun, view, price), or taking the next step?
- What would we have to learn from users to know it's worth building?
- What data does it need, and can EIER give us any?
- Who in the team can build it? (No UX/design student; frontend and privacy are strengths.)
- Privacy: personalisation and AI touch personal data; a natural role for the cybersecurity members.

### 6. Scrum setup: decisions to make

- **Definition of Done draft** for the increment, separate from story acceptance criteria [L2 pp. 10–13].
- **Team agreement:** meeting cadence, stand-up time/format, how we handle lateness or absence [L4 slide 10 asks exactly this], how we decide.
- **Risk register** owner. First risks: no UX/design student; PO availability; composition rule; one-week sprints; exam season in December.
- **Scrumwise:** who builds the first product backlog (epics from the case). Story points are 0–5; anything bigger gets split [L2 p. 34]. Optional: a short planning-poker practice round [L2 pp. 35–36].
- **Evidence log** for the reflection notes: one shared doc where we note decisions, obstacles, and changes.
- **Retrospective minutes** are a required deliverable for every sprint [L4 slide 45]. Agree a template now.
- Where product code lives, and whether the prototype is Figma, frontend, or both.

## Expected outcome

A shortlist of directions, a research plan with an owner, a PO contact plan, and owners for DoD, team agreement, risk register and Scrumwise backlog, all with dates before the Design Sprint.
