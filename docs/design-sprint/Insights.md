## Key insights [interp]

1. **The buyer buys something that doesn't exist yet.** The barrier is uncertainty and gut feeling; sun, view and layout replace the viewing. Matches research themes 1, 2 and 9 (`docs/research/Findings.md`).
2. **Overview first, detail later.** The cinema seat map ("hele salen visuelt, ledige seter i farge, trykker for å velge") is a pattern people understand without a tutorial.
3. **Filtering is weak in today's unit pickers** (Vabu Park: "Altfor dårlig filteringer"). Hard requirements should give a short list, not 80 units.
4. **Comparison is missing.** Tesla's side-by-side of model variants has no equivalent in home buying. Matches Findings (5 ask outright).
5. **Checkout is too heavy.** Tesla shows a big purchase can be done with full overview of price, delivery, deposit (Vipps) and a simple add-on list, with all documents in one app.
6. **Economy must be visual.** Savings / egenkapital is a selling point but must be shown "uten mye tekst". Matches Findings theme 4 (total cost hidden, 10/21) and theme 14 (jargon).
7. **Trust comes from transparency and a plain, personal tone** (Joone, Apto, Skin City).
8. **Projects change.** Buyers need updates during construction, not only at sale. Matches Findings theme 2 (11/21).

### Lightning-critique worksheet (one member's five building blocks, summarised)

1. 3D model of the surroundings (like Løkeberggrenda), sun lines (like Plyo) and the balcony view.
2. Top-down view with SunCalc and other info.
3. Quick checkout inspired by Tesla's minimalism; all documents and contracts in one app; "ingen behov for meglerkontakt".
4. Detailed floor plan with colour and furniture, 3–4 furnishing variants per unit; possibly generated from needs ("Må ha stort spisebord med 8 plasser", "Fokus på stor TV-stue").
5. More detailed filtering and clearer comparison.
